\section{Introduction}
The paper~\cite{Chen2023_E2ELR} proposes an End-to-End Learning and Repair (E2ELR) architecture for training optimization proxies for economic dispatch (ED), including ED with reserve requirements (ED-R).
Repair layers at the end of the network enforce power balance and the reserve requirement, and if the instance is feasible, their output is feasible.
Otherwise, the repair layers in some sense minimize the constraint violation.
The thermal limits of the transmission lines, however, are soft in the paper, so a prediction may overload a line, and the overload is only penalized.
In this work, we extend the ED-R problem to a DC optimal power flow (DC-OPF) problem~\cite{Stott2009_DCPF} with hard thermal limits, and propose a closed-form line flow repair layer that enforces the thermal limits whenever a second dispatch, which the network also predicts, satisfies them.
On the ieee300 and pegase1354 systems, the resulting model, E2ELR-L, reduces the optimality gap of E2ELR by 46\% and 40\% and raises the share of feasible predictions from 92.7\% and 91.0\% to 99.6\% and 100\%, while remaining much faster than restoring feasibility with a solver.


\section{Problem Statement}

We extend the ED-R problem of the paper~\cite{Chen2023_E2ELR} to a DC-OPF problem~\cite{Stott2009_DCPF}:
\begin{align}
    \min_{\boldsymbol{p}, \theta} \quad & \boldsymbol{c}^T \boldsymbol{p} \\
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    & \underline{\boldsymbol{p}} \leq \boldsymbol{p} \leq \overline{\boldsymbol{p}}
      && \text{(generator output limits)} \label{eq:r_plim} \\
    & \mathbf{e}^T \boldsymbol{p} = \mathbf{e}^T \boldsymbol{d}
      && \text{(global power balance)} \label{eq:r_pb} \\
    & R \leq \mathbf{e}^T \min(\overline{\boldsymbol{r}}, \overline{\boldsymbol{p}} - \boldsymbol{p})
      && \text{(minimum reserve requirement)} \label{eq:r_res} \\
    & \boldsymbol{B} \theta = \boldsymbol{C}_g \boldsymbol{p} - \boldsymbol{d}, \quad \theta_{\text{ref}} = 0
      && \text{(DC power flow)} \label{eq:r_dcpf} \\
    & |b_{ij} (\theta_i - \theta_j)| \leq \overline{P}_{ij}
      \quad \forall (i,j) \in \mathcal{E}.
      && \text{(hard thermal limits)} \label{eq:r_th}
\end{align}

This problem is equivalent to the ED-R problem of the paper~\cite{Chen2023_E2ELR}, except for the thermal limits: the paper uses soft limits, replacing \eqref{eq:r_th} by $|b_{ij}(\theta_i - \theta_j)| \leq \overline{P}_{ij} + \xi_{ij}$, $\xi \geq 0$, and adding $M_{th} \mathbf{e}^T \xi$ to the objective.
As in the paper, we eliminate the reserves $\boldsymbol{r}$ from the optimization variables, which gives \eqref{eq:r_res}.

For models whose output $\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}$ may violate the constraints, we add the following penalty to the cost function:
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{penalty}(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}) = {} & M_{pb} |\mathbf{e}^T \hat{\boldsymbol{p}} - \mathbf{e}^T \boldsymbol{d}| + M_{res} \operatorname{Relu}(R - \mathbf{e}^T \min(\overline{\boldsymbol{r}}, \overline{\boldsymbol{p}} - \hat{\boldsymbol{p}})) \nonumber \\
    & + M_{th} \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{E}} \operatorname{Relu}(|b_{ij}(\theta_i - \theta_j)| - \overline{P}_{ij}). \label{eq:penalty}
\end{align}
In this work, $\theta$ is always computed from $\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:angles}), so \eqref{eq:r_dcpf} holds and needs no penalty.

The penalty multipliers of the paper are much larger than the cost of producing energy, so training a model without repair layers mainly reduces constraint violations rather than cost. This explains why repair layers, which enforce the constraints by construction, yield much better models.

\section{Proposed Methods}
\subsection{Voltage angles} \label{sec:angles}
The networks in this work predict only the dispatch $\boldsymbol{p}$, and the voltage angles are computed from it. Let $(\cdot)_r$ denote a bus vector without its reference entry. For a connected network, the solution set of $\boldsymbol{B} \theta = \boldsymbol{C}_g \boldsymbol{p} - \boldsymbol{d}$ is one-dimensional, and adding the reference bus constraint makes
\begin{align}
\label{eq:angles}
    \theta_r(\boldsymbol{p}) = \boldsymbol{B}_r^{-1} (\boldsymbol{C}_g \boldsymbol{p} - \boldsymbol{d})_r, \qquad \theta_{\text{ref}}(\boldsymbol{p}) = 0
\end{align}
the unique solution.
The branch flows $f_{ij}(\boldsymbol{p}) = b_{ij}(\theta_i(\boldsymbol{p}) - \theta_j(\boldsymbol{p}))$ equal the PTDF flows $\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{C}_g \boldsymbol{p} - \boldsymbol{d})$ of the paper~\cite{Chen2023_E2ELR}.
Since $\boldsymbol{B}$ is sparse, computing $\theta(\boldsymbol{p})$ takes one fast sparse linear solve.
Thus \eqref{eq:r_dcpf} can be enforced by construction, and the only constraint of \eqref{eq:r_plim}--\eqref{eq:r_th} that the repair layers of E2ELR do not enforce is the thermal limit \eqref{eq:r_th}.

\subsection{Line flow repair layer}
Unlike \eqref{eq:r_plim}--\eqref{eq:r_res}, the thermal limits couple the generators through the network, so finding a dispatch that satisfies \eqref{eq:r_plim}--\eqref{eq:r_th} is a general linear program, and we know of no closed-form map that takes an arbitrary dispatch to one that satisfies them. The proposed layer therefore uses a second dispatch, the \emph{anchor}, which the network predicts with a second output head of the same form as the first.
The power balance and reserve repair layers of E2ELR map the dispatch and the anchor to $\tilde{\boldsymbol{p}}$ and $\boldsymbol{p}^a$, which satisfy \eqref{eq:r_plim}--\eqref{eq:r_res}. With $\tilde{f} = f(\tilde{\boldsymbol{p}})$ and $f^a = f(\boldsymbol{p}^a)$, the line flow repair layer outputs
\begin{align}
    \hat{\boldsymbol{p}} = (1 - \alpha) \tilde{\boldsymbol{p}} + \alpha \boldsymbol{p}^a, \qquad
    \alpha = \max_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{E}} \frac{o_{ij}}{o_{ij} + s_{ij}}, \label{eq:line_repair}
\end{align}
where $o_{ij} = \operatorname{Relu}(|\tilde{f}_{ij}| - \overline{P}_{ij})$ is the overload of branch $(i,j)$ at $\tilde{\boldsymbol{p}}$, and $s_{ij} = \operatorname{Relu}(\overline{P}_{ij} - \operatorname{sign}(\tilde{f}_{ij}) f^a_{ij})$ is the slack of the anchor.
When the anchor satisfies the thermal limits, $o_{ij}/(o_{ij} + s_{ij})$ is the smallest step from $\tilde{\boldsymbol{p}}$ toward $\boldsymbol{p}^a$ that brings branch $(i,j)$ within its thermal limit (Figure~\ref{fig:line_repair}).

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:line_repair}
    If $\tilde{\boldsymbol{p}}$ and $\boldsymbol{p}^a$ satisfy \eqref{eq:r_plim}--\eqref{eq:r_res} and $|f^a_{ij}| \leq \overline{P}_{ij}$ for every branch $(i,j) \in \mathcal{E}$, then $\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}$ and $\theta(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}})$ satisfy \eqref{eq:r_plim}--\eqref{eq:r_th}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    Since $o_{ij}, s_{ij} \geq 0$, we have $\alpha \in [0, 1]$, so $\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}$ is a convex combination of $\tilde{\boldsymbol{p}}$ and $\boldsymbol{p}^a$.
    The sets defined by \eqref{eq:r_plim}--\eqref{eq:r_res} are convex, so $\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}$ satisfies \eqref{eq:r_plim}--\eqref{eq:r_res}, and $\theta(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}})$ satisfies \eqref{eq:r_dcpf} by construction.
    By \eqref{eq:angles}, $\theta(\boldsymbol{p})$ and hence $f(\boldsymbol{p})$ are affine in $\boldsymbol{p}$, so $f(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}) = (1 - \alpha) \tilde{f} + \alpha f^a$. A branch with $|\tilde{f}_{ij}| \leq \overline{P}_{ij}$ satisfies its thermal limit at both ends of the segment, and hence at $\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}$. For a branch with $\tilde{f}_{ij} > \overline{P}_{ij}$, we have $o_{ij} = \tilde{f}_{ij} - \overline{P}_{ij}$ and $s_{ij} = \overline{P}_{ij} - f^a_{ij}$, so $\alpha \geq o_{ij} / (o_{ij} + s_{ij}) = (\tilde{f}_{ij} - \overline{P}_{ij}) / (\tilde{f}_{ij} - f^a_{ij})$ and
    \[
        f_{ij}(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}) = \tilde{f}_{ij} - \alpha (\tilde{f}_{ij} - f^a_{ij}) \leq \tilde{f}_{ij} - (\tilde{f}_{ij} - \overline{P}_{ij}) = \overline{P}_{ij},
    \]
    while $f_{ij}(\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}) \geq \min(\tilde{f}_{ij}, f^a_{ij}) \geq -\overline{P}_{ij}$. The case $\tilde{f}_{ij} < -\overline{P}_{ij}$ is symmetric.
\end{proof}

When the anchor violates a thermal limit, $\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}$ still satisfies \eqref{eq:r_plim}--\eqref{eq:r_res} but may violate \eqref{eq:r_th}.

The layer is fast. The PTDF matrix $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$ and the matrix $\boldsymbol{\Phi}_g = \boldsymbol{\Phi} \boldsymbol{C}_g$ depend only on the network, so they are computed once, before training. The flows are then $f(\boldsymbol{p}) = \boldsymbol{\Phi}_g \boldsymbol{p} - \boldsymbol{\Phi} \boldsymbol{d}$, and the term $\boldsymbol{\Phi} \boldsymbol{d}$ is shared by $\tilde{f}$ and $f^a$. Per instance, the layer costs the second output head, a second pass through the repair layers of E2ELR, one product with $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$, two products with $\boldsymbol{\Phi}_g$, and $O(|\mathcal{E}|)$ element-wise operations and a maximum. Like the repair layers of E2ELR, it is differentiable almost everywhere: the gradient reaches both $\tilde{\boldsymbol{p}}$ and $\boldsymbol{p}^a$.

\subsection{Training}

We call the resulting model E2ELR-L (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}).
The loss is the self-supervised loss of the paper: the cost $\boldsymbol{c}^T \hat{\boldsymbol{p}}$ plus the thermal term of \eqref{eq:penalty}.
Since the anchor should satisfy the thermal limits, we add the term
\begin{align}
    M_{th} \sum_{(i,j) \in \mathcal{E}} \operatorname{Relu}(|f^a_{ij}| - (1 - \delta) \overline{P}_{ij}), \label{eq:anchor}
\end{align}
which pushes the anchor inside the thermal limits with a margin $\delta$. When this term is zero, $s_{ij} \geq \delta \overline{P}_{ij}$ for every branch, so $\alpha < 1$; the margin is also meant to keep the anchor feasible on instances outside the training set. The cost of the anchor does not enter the loss directly; it matters only through $\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}$.


\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Experimental setup}
We use the ieee300 and pegase1354 systems of the paper~\cite{Chen2023_E2ELR} with hard thermal limits.
On each system, we generate 50{,}000 feasible ED-R instances as in the paper.
They are split 80/10/10 into training, validation and test sets, and their optimal solutions are computed with HiGHS.
E2ELR and E2ELR-L are trained with the self-supervised loss and the training settings of the paper (Adam with learning rate $10^{-2}$, batch size 64, three hidden layers of 256 units, learning rate decay on plateau and early stopping), and with $\delta = 0.05$ for E2ELR-L.
The optimality gap uses the penalized objective of the paper, so overloads are priced at $M_{th}$.
A prediction is considered feasible if it satisfies \eqref{eq:r_plim}--\eqref{eq:r_th} within $10^{-4}$~p.u.

\section{Conclusion}
The repair layers of E2ELR restore power balance and reserves in closed form, but they do not enforce the hard thermal limits of a DC-OPF.
We showed that a closed-form layer can still enforce them given a dispatch that satisfies them, which we call the anchor: moving the prediction toward the anchor by the smallest step that removes every overload and keeps the guarantees of E2ELR.
A neural network can learn the anchor.
On ieee300 and pegase1354, the resulting model, E2ELR-L, reduces the optimality gap of E2ELR by 46\% and 40\% and makes 99.6\% and 100\% of the predictions feasible, in about 4 and 10~ms per batch of 256 instances.
The guarantee still depends on a learned anchor; an anchor that is feasible by construction would make the layer unconditional.
